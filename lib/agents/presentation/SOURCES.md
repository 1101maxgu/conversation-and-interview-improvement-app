# Presentation Q&A agent: sources

Mostly practitioner guides and blogs, plus one working paper. Nothing here is peer-reviewed Q&A research. Everything in knowledge.ts is distilled and reworded, not copied. Found via web search (standard mode); search summaries were used, not full-page reads.

- Question types (clarifying, hypothetical, multi-part, off-topic, hostile, comment disguised as a question, unanswerable): University of Edinburgh Q&A handout; SlideGenius Q&A articles; 3D Universal English Institute Q&A guide.
- Hostile questions (acknowledge, reframe neutrally, answer the substance, bridge back, do not argue): Acuity Training "Handling difficult questions"; HighSpark hard-questions guide; Dooley Communications tough-questions series.
- "I don't know" (admit, bridge to what you do know, promise and deliver a follow-up): entrepreneurship.org "Tough Questions"; Maurizio Lacava Q&A FAQ; SlideGenius.
- Executive questions (cost, ROI, payback, opportunity cost, risk versus reward, blunt rapid-fire as a sign of interest): Association for Talent Development "Presenting to the Big Dogs"; Aakash Gupta on presenting to executives; Work It Daily.
- Technical and research audiences (method, data, assumptions, limitations, prior-work comparison, generalisability, next steps; admit limits then state scope): AI Scholar conference Q&A guide; Enago Academy; University of Waterloo (D. Harder) technical presentation guidelines; DevConf speaker guide.
- Audience reaction to dodging: Rogers and Norton, "The Artful Dodger" (Harvard Business School working paper, 2008): answering a different question costs likability and trust, a well-executed dodge costs little but is still worse than answering. Via secondary summaries, single study.

## Known limits
- Heuristics in index.ts read wording only; they cannot tell whether an answer actually fits the question.
- Audience behaviour for hostile and executive groups is distilled advice, not measured data.
