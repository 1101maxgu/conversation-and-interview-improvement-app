# Friend agent sources

Distilled in our own words (nothing copied) from public material found on 2026-10-11. Most sources are practitioner, university-wellbeing, clinic-blog or popular-psychology pieces, not primary research. Treat the rules as sensible defaults, not clinical guidance. No fine-tuning: "trained" here means distilled rules, worked examples and per-turn hints in `knowledge.ts`.

Topics and what we took from them

- Active listening, reflecting and validation (Victoria University of Wellington wellbeing guide, Lyra Health, teaching resources): give full attention, paraphrase, name the feeling tentatively ("sounds like"), and validate that it makes sense without agreeing with everything. A wrong label is fine; people correct it.
- Advice vs support (Chen et al. 1999 on unasked-for support, PubMed 10224636; Vanderbilt news on unsolicited help; teen autonomy studies): unasked support and advice can imply incompetence and raise stress, purely emotional support lands better. Evidence is mostly adjacent contexts, not controlled friend-venting studies.
- Responding to venting (assorted self-help and psychiatrist-quoted articles): validate first, do not fix or minimise ("at least..."), ask "advice or just listening?", ask what they need.
- Good news (Shelly Gable's active-constructive responding, summarised on Wikipedia, Psychology Today and study abstracts): enthusiastic, curious responses to good news go with better wellbeing and relationship satisfaction. Mostly couples research, one social-media study for friends.
- Humour (Colorado State caregiver study, Psychology Today pieces, counselling articles): humour that connects or eases tension helps, humour that deflects unacknowledged pain or mocks tends to dismiss feelings. Evidence is suggestive.
- Friend vs therapist boundaries (Talkspace, Symmetry Counseling, Tava Health, Columbia Chronicle): a friend is not a therapist, recurring heavy topics are a cue to warmly point toward other support, and the nudge is framed as care, not a brush-off.
- Loneliness (Crisis Text Line, Lifeline Australia, Young Scot, HealthLink BC): listen more than fix, avoid "just go meet people", make small low-pressure offers, accept "I'm fine" while leaving the door open.
- Gap: the search found little on "blunt but caring" candour, so that vibe rests on the validate-first-then-honest pattern plus the ask-before-advising finding, not on a dedicated source.
